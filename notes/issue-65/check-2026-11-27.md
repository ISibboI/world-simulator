# #65 check on main e55bf1d (2026-11-27)

Question: is tropical lowland still colder than the sea of its latitude, by how much, why, and is
that physics or a model deficiency?

**Answer: yes, and more than before. It is a model deficiency, not physics.** The cause is the land
albedo combined with the model's lack of tropical atmospheric heat transport into land. The albedo
values themselves are plausible.

## 1. Measurement

Definition as in #33 (review finding 1): fine cells at |lat| < 25°, land 0–300 m without lakes,
annual mean surface temperature minus the mean sea surface (ocean cells) of the same 5° band.
Snapshots 500–1000 Myr, 8 seeds × 1000 Myr, `--cell-dump --snapshot-interval 100`
([`gap.py`](gap.py)). The runs are runs `r1127-232452-8033` and `r1127-232452-5022` on main e55bf1d.

| case | gap < 300 m (K) | per seed | at sea level (lapse removed) | < 500 m | coast cells | inland cells | inland, P ≥ 1 m/yr | inland, P < 0.25 m/yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| default (barren, epoch 0, land albedo 0.37) | **−10.9** | −9.8 … −13.1 | −10.3 | −11.8 | −6.3 | −11.7 | −3.9 | −15.0 |
| `--biosphere modern --epoch 4` (land albedo 0.32) | **−7.1** | −5.9 … −9.6 | −6.3 | −8.2 | −4.9 | −7.7 | −3.4 | −11.2 |

(The "coast" and "inland" columns are fine coast cells and all other land. The wet and dry columns
cover all of that land, split by precipitation.)

Absolute means (|lat| < 25°, unweighted): default lowland 19.2 °C against sea 29.8 °C; modern
lowland 30.6 °C against sea 37.8 °C. The modern runs end at epoch 5 with a 9% brighter sun, so
their tropics are hot. That is a separate matter and not part of this issue.

- main cf409e1 (the #14 runs, before #217) gives the same picture: modern −7.0 K, default −9.3 K.
  #217 changed only the start and the plates, not the temperature model.
- #33 reported −4.6…−5.3 K on the then-default (modern, epoch 4, fixed forcing). The temperature
  coefficients (`land_albedo`, transport) have not changed since. The larger gap now comes from
  the barren preset (albedo 0.37) and, for modern, from the carbon cycle and the brighter sun of
  later epochs (more insolation times the same albedo contrast). Continent geometry also plays a
  part. I did not split this further.
- The lapse rate is not the explanation. Lowland < 300 m averages 100–130 m, which is 0.7–0.9 K.

## 2. Cause: energy budget

The model (`src/temperature.rs`) gives every coarse cell (subdivision 4, ~440 km) a land surface
and an ocean column:
`C dT/dt = Q(1−α) − (A − F + B T) + band transport + local 2-D diffusion + land–ocean exchange
(+ currents for the ocean)`. There is no latent or sensible heat term, and clouds are not
separate: α is a fixed planetary albedo (land 0.32, abiotic 0.37; tropical ocean
0.31 − 0.09 = 0.22). The band transport adds the same W/m² to every cell of a latitude band.
Everything is linear in T in the tropics, so the annual-mean gap splits exactly into terms divided
by B = 2.09 W/m²/K.

A test-only probe ([`probe.diff`](probe.diff), `cfg(test)` accumulator plus an ignored test, no
change to normal builds) reruns the temperature model on the 1000-Myr cell dumps with each seed's
forcing. It reproduces the dumped gap (−11.84 against −11.87 K default, −7.54 against −7.51 K
modern; [`probe-variants.txt`](probe-variants.txt)). Coarse cells ≥ 50% land at |lat| < 25°
(on average 96% land) against the open sea of the same model band, means of 8 seeds:

| term (K) | default | modern |
|---|---|---|
| ΔT land − sea (coarse) | −12.8 | −8.8 |
| absorbed sunlight, Q Δ(1−α)/B | **−20.5** | **−17.8** |
| local 2-D diffusion from neighbouring air | +6.6 | +6.6 |
| land–ocean exchange in the cell | +0.2 | +0.2 |
| ocean currents (cool the tropical sea) | +1.0 | +2.1 |
| band transport | 0 | 0 |

So the albedo contrast alone would make a large continent 18–20 K colder than the sea. The model's
only horizontal coupling that can bring extra heat to colder land is the weak 2-D diffusion
(`local_heat_transport` = 0.05). Its reach is √(D/B) ≈ 0.15 rad ≈ 1,000 km, so it recovers only a
third. The land–ocean exchange works only inside a coarse cell. Continental interiors at
≥ 440 km have almost no ocean, so the exchange does nothing for them. This explains the pattern
in the table: coastal lowland is −5 to −6 K, inland −8 to −12 K.

The wet/dry split is geography, not physics. Precipitation does not enter the energy balance
(only ice sheets via snowfall), so dry land is colder only because it is inland. Real dry land
would be *warmer* (no evaporative cooling), the opposite sign.

Sensitivities (same probe, 8 seeds):

| change | default gap | modern gap |
|---|---|---|
| none | −11.8 | −7.5 |
| `land_albedo` = 0.27 | −1.9 | −1.7 |
| `land_albedo` = 0.22 (= tropical sea) | +3.0 | +4.3 |
| `land_ocean_exchange` 10 → 40 | −11.8 | −7.5 |
| `local_heat_transport` 0.05 → 0.2 | −9.3 | −5.6 |
| `local_heat_transport` 0.05 → 0.45 | −7.4 | −4.6 |

With equal albedo, land would be +3…+4 K warmer than the sea, because the sea loses heat to the
currents.

## 3. Physics or model?

- **The albedo is plausible.** Earth's top-of-atmosphere albedo is ~0.30–0.40 over the Sahara and
  Arabia, ~0.20–0.25 over tropical rain forest (with its clouds) and ~0.15–0.25 over tropical
  oceans. Bare rock and sand on a barren planet sit at the high end, so 0.37 is reasonable for a
  barren tropical land surface, and 0.32 is a reasonable average over forest and desert.
- **The cold land is not plausible.** On Earth the bright deserts are *radiative sinks* at the top
  of the atmosphere (the Sahara has a net deficit of the order of tens of W/m²). They are still
  warmer than the sea of their latitude: Sahara annual means 25–30 °C against ~22–24 °C SST at
  20–25°N. Tropical forest is about as warm as the sea. The deficit is covered by atmospheric heat
  import: subsidence in the Hadley/Walker circulation, and the weak-temperature-gradient balance
  of the tropics. Gravity waves keep the free troposphere almost uniform along latitude, so a
  bright patch cannot get much colder than its surroundings. The surface below dry air is then
  even warmer, because it does not evaporate. The model has neither mechanism. Every coarse cell
  must balance its own albedo deficit with its own OLR at B = 2 W/m²/K.
- **Not a grid effect (#64).** The bias grows with distance from the coast over 1,000+ km. That is
  the model's transport length, not the 110 km (or 440 km temperature) mesh. The coarse mesh only
  smears the coast: coastal fine cells interpolate a land column that is mostly land, so they show
  −5 to −6 K instead of ~0.
- For a barren planet the right picture is: bright land lowers the *planet's* absorbed energy (a
  real global cooling, which the model has), but the tropical atmosphere spreads that cooling over
  land and sea alike. It does not concentrate it on the land.

**Verdict:** a model deficiency (missing tropical atmospheric heat convergence into land), larger
than when #33 found it: −11 K barren, −7 K modern. It matters beyond the map colours. Tropical land
temperature drives silicate weathering (so the carbon cycle), the ice-sheet criterion, evaporation
and the Köppen classes (tropical A climates need ≥ 18 °C in the coldest month).

## 4. Options

1. **Tropical band coupling (recommended).** Relax every surface (land, sea, lake) towards the
   mean air of its latitude band with a coupling k, full to 20° and fading out by 35°. This is the
   weak-temperature-gradient mechanism. It conserves energy within the band, since the sea gives
   what the land gets. I tried it test-only in the probe ([`probe-band-coupling.txt`](probe-band-coupling.txt),
   8 seeds, 1000 Myr dumps):

   | k (W/m²/K) | default gap | modern gap | lowland 10–25° seasonal range (K) | global mean |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 0 (today) | −11.8 | −7.5 | 14.5 / 15.8 | 17.9 / 23.6 °C |
   | 5 | −5.2 | −3.9 | 11.1 / 12.7 | −0.2 / −0.3 K |
   | 10 | −3.6 | −2.9 | 9.1 / 10.7 | −0.2 / −0.5 K |
   | 20 | −2.4 | −2.2 | 6.9 / 8.3 | −0.4 / −0.7 K |

   k ≈ 10 removes ~70% of the bias and keeps a monsoon-like seasonal cycle of ~10 K (Earth's
   tropical land at 10–25°: ~2–5 K in rain forest, 10–15 K in the Sahel and Sahara). Effort:
   small to medium. It needs ~30 lines in `update`, a parameter, a unit test (a tropical continent
   within ~3 K of its band sea), and an 8-seed check of the side effects (weathering and CO₂,
   precipitation, ice, Köppen). The remaining −2…−3 K is the missing surface latent heat, which
   would warm dry land. That would be a later, larger step (a surface energy budget) and is not
   needed now.
   Caveat: the coupling is instantaneous, so it also damps tropical seasons. Coupling only the
   annual-mean anomaly would avoid that, but then energy is no longer conserved.
2. **Extend the free-air shift (#33) down to sea level.** This is a parameter-sized change
   (`free_air_base` plus a floor weight), but it is a post-hoc shift that does not conserve energy.
   It would also reopen #33 review finding 2. Not recommended.
3. **Lower the land albedo.** It works numerically (0.27 gives ~−2 K), but it is the wrong lever:
   the albedos are plausible, and it would warm the whole planet. Not recommended.
4. **Raise `local_heat_transport`.** It is weak even at 9× (−7.4 / −4.6 K), and it would tie
   mid- and high-latitude continents to the sea, which loses continentality. Not recommended.
5. **Accept it.** Possible, but the bias feeds into weathering and the carbon cycle.

Recommendation: option 1 with k ≈ 10 W/m²/K, as its own issue/PR. The 8-seed side-effect check
should come before anyone tunes it.
