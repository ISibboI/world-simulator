# #37 check, 2026-11-26: are slab dynamic-topography depressions still too deep?

**Short answer: yes, still relevant, and not better than in #34.** The code that makes the
field is unchanged since #34 (`src/tectonics/mantle.rs::dynamic_topography`, slab loads from
`dynamics.rs::sinking_slabs`), and the subduction flux that feeds it is still ~2× Earth's
(#32 is open).

## What the code does on main (1418167)

- `h = −f · M · K(θ) / ρ_m` per slab-grid vertex (normalised Gaussian `K`), three depth stages
  with response `f` = 0.5 / 0.4 / 0.2, widths σ = 600 / 900 / 1500 km, residences
  13 / 45 / 67 Myr, plus plume swells (≤ 650 m dry, σ 450 km). Zero global mean.
  `mantle.topography_response = 1.0`. Unchanged since #34 apart from the trig-free math (#79).
- Slab mass flux per trench cell = relative convergence of the two **plate rotations** × cell
  boundary length × `(ρ_m − ρ_w)(d(age) − d(0))`. The thin viscous sheet (#163) does not enter
  here: the deformation velocity of non-rigid plates is ignored for the slab flux (it is used
  in the boundary couplings). A small inconsistency, not the cause of the depth.
- Nothing from #163/#165/#168/#182 changed the response itself.

## Comparison with Earth

- Observed residual (dynamic) topography: Hoggard et al. 2016 (Nat. Geosci.) find
  amplitudes up to about ±1 km in the oceans (water-loaded, i.e. ~±0.7 km dry) and only
  ~±0.5 km at the longest wavelengths (degrees 1–3), much less than the ±2 km predicted by
  many mantle-flow models. Flament et al. 2013 (Lithosphere) review predicted amplitudes of
  ±1–2 km and inferred continental flooding/subsidence of a few hundred metres up to ~1 km
  (e.g. the Western Interior Seaway over the Farallon slab).
- Model: 1st percentile −1.6 km dry, extremes −2.1 (typical snapshot) to −3.0 km dry,
  i.e. ~2–3× the observed bound. An Earth-like planet is not held to Earth's numbers, but
  > 2 km of dry deflection is beyond what any plausible slab load/viscosity combination gives.

## Cause

1. **Subduction flux ~1.6–2× Earth's (#32 open).** The field scales linearly with it (#34's
   half-response run halved the extremes). At an Earth-like flux the depressions would be about
   −1.0…−1.3 km dry at the deepest snapshot point and the 1st percentile ~−0.9 km: at the upper
   edge of observations, still deeper than Hoggard's long-wavelength bound.
2. **The response per unit slab mass is at the high end.** The three-stage responses
   (0.5/0.4/0.2) are taken as fractions of the weight supported at the surface, with no
   thermal assimilation of slabs (the excess mass does not decay while the slab sinks), no
   compensation by the deflected 660 km phase boundary, and kernels that are Gaussians of
   fixed width. Real kernels for a lower mantle 30–100× more viscous are smaller at the
   long wavelengths where most of the field's power is (correlation length ~2900 km).
3. Deeper than #34 reported: same code, similar flux (6.0 vs 6.1 km²/yr). The difference is
   likely the measure (#34 quoted one extreme over 8 seeds; here per-seed extremes are
   −2.2…−3.0 km, mean per-snapshot minimum −2.1 km) and trenches that now stay longer in place
   (plates are non-rigid since #163, so slab mass accumulates under fewer slab-grid vertices).
   I did not test the second part.

**Grid (#64):** not a cause. The cells (~110 km) are much finer than the kernels
(σ 600–1500 km) and the slab grid (~900 km). The grid does make boundaries zig-zag, which the
trench length already corrects (×1/1.1); the excess trench length of #32 is a model cause
(every converging oceanic contact becomes a trench), not a grid one.

## Fix proposal

- **A. Fix the flux first (#32).** Lowers the depressions by ~40% with no change to this code.
  Effort: open-ended (#32 has been investigated twice without a single cause).
- **B. Viscosity-structure-based response (the plan of #37), independent of A.**
  - Response kernels from a layered viscosity profile (lithosphere, upper mantle, lower mantle
    30× more viscous; Hager 1984 / Richards & Hager 1984 propagator), applied per spherical
    harmonic degree or approximated by per-stage response and width that depend on wavelength;
  - thermal assimilation: the slab's excess mass decays with time in the lower mantle;
  - optional: 660 km phase-boundary compensation for the upper-mantle stage.
  - Effort: ~1–2 agent days including an 8-seed × 1000 Myr evaluation. Risk: #34's cover
    budget depends on the amplitude (half response: sediment-part slope 2.2 → 10.3 m/100 Myr),
    so the #21 target must be re-checked.
- **C. Quick tuning:** `mantle.topography_response = 0.5`. Gives −0.9…−1.2 km extremes now,
  but is tuning, against the project's preference for physics.

Recommendation: B, after or together with whatever #32 brings; keep the issue open.
