- Same definitions as the original measurement (`notes/issue-5/results.md` F4 on
  `agent/relief-5-7`), i.e. the model's own log line:
  - endorheic land: land whose water ends in a closed depression that does not spill;
  - "single-cell pit": a closed depression of one cell (cells at least 1 m below the spill level);
  - lakes: covered cells (fractional at the shore), spilling or not;
  - lake evaporation: evaporation of lakes beyond the evapotranspiration of the land they cover.

## Verdict per point

1. **Single-cell pits as closed basins — resolved.** Land draining into one-cell depressions is
   0.57% of land (5% of the endorheic land), down from ~45% of it; with 2–3-cell depressions
   1.3%. 77% of the endorheic land drains into basins of more than 10 cells. Probably the work
   of #165 (one-to-one resampling, pit and twin drained as one, deep holes healed) and #166
   (lakes from the end of the step with an onset depth); not attributed in detail. #172 (pits
   ~30–40% of endorheic land) quotes numbers that no longer hold on main.
2. **Endorheic share — no longer a problem.** 12% of land (4–21% between seeds) against Earth's
   18–20%. On an Earth-like planet the share follows the extent of arid interiors, which differs
   between seeds; the endorheic land is now real multi-cell basins. Occasional snapshots with up
   to 61% are cut-off ocean basins whose surrounding land drains into them (#180).
3. **Lake area — partly.** 3.8% of land (target 0.5–2%; Earth ~1.8% for lakes ≳ 1 km², ~3.7%
   counting all small lakes, Verpoorter et al. 2014). Split by floor: lakes on floors above sea
   level 2.3% (median 1.8%), at the upper edge of the target; the rest are cut-off ocean basins
   below sea level, which the default without the 0.2% rule produces (#180, a grid effect: on
   Earth a strait narrower than a cell would keep them connected, #64). "Every spilling
   depression counts as fully covered" is still the definition, but it is physical: a lake that
   overflows is full to its sill. The grid effect is that a depression ≥ 1 m below its spill
   level is a lake over whole ~12,000 km² cells (15% of lake cells are < 10 m deep). Partial
   cover for spilling lakes is therefore not recommended.
4. **Lake evaporation — higher than before, causes known.** 6.8 × 10³ km³/yr; lakes on floors
   above sea level 2.5 × 10³ (median 1.9 × 10³), in the old range. The rest comes from cut-off
   basins (4.2 × 10³), 82% of all lake evaporation is within 30° of the equator, and sea-fed
   basins evaporate 1.0 × 10³ beyond their own inflow. The excess per lake area (1.0 m/yr) is
   plausible; the total is set by the cut-off seas and by too-warm tropical lakes, which is
   #184 (lake evaporation missing from the lake energy balance).
5. **Inner sills / Fill-Spill-Merge — resolved by #168.** Per snapshot 8.8 depressions have
   several local minima and 4.4 hold separate lakes below inner sills. Remaining simplification:
   sediment still fills a depression from its lowest floor, whatever sub-basin it arrives in
   (#168 caveat); minor.

## Recommendation

Close #46: the pits and the inner sills are fixed; what remains is covered elsewhere.
- Cut-off ocean basins (lake area, evaporation, endorheic spikes): the open decision of #180
  (rule off as now; `--set min_sea_fraction=0.002`; or a sub-grid strait). Effort: none for
  the switch, ~1 day for a strait rule.
- Too-warm tropical lakes: #184 (~1–2 days).
- #172 can be re-checked and probably closed with these numbers (0.57% of land into one-cell pits).
- Optional, not recommended now: a sub-cell lake fraction from the depression hypsometry, so that
  shallow wide depressions do not count as whole lake cells (~2–3 days; changes climate input).

The diagnostic (commit 166827b) is not in a PR.
