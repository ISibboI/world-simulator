# #14 re-check on main cf409e1 (2026-11-27): is ancient sea floor still a problem?

Question (Sebastian, 2026-11-27): "is this still a problem?"

## Setup

- `main` cf409e1 (after #195 plume physics, #211, #196 carbon cycle, #212 escape, epoch-0 barren default).
  No code change; only `--cell-dump`.
- 8 seeds × 1000 Myr, two configurations:
  - **default**: abiotic, epoch 0 (the current default run),
  - **modern**: `--biosphere modern --epoch 4` (today's Earth, the default before #190).
- Cell dumps every 100 Myr. All numbers are averaged over the 6 snapshots at 500–1000 Myr
  (48 snapshots per configuration).
- Script: [`ancient.py`](ancient.py) (per-snapshot analysis), [`summ.py`](summ.py) (summary).
  Runs: `r1127-210228-0387-issue-14` (default) and `r1127-210228-f948-issue-14` (modern).

Definitions, as in the issue and PR #23:

- **Sea floor**: oceanic basement (crust class 2) under the sea (ocean mask). This is the same as
  the logged `sea floor: … ≥ 200 Myr` metric. The script matches the log to ±0.05 percentage
  points.
- **Region**: connected sea-floor cells of one plate.
  - *Enclosed basin*: a region with no plate-boundary cell.
  - *Marginal/inland sea*: open to a boundary, but ≥ 50% of the rim is the plate's own
    land, continental crust or arc crust, and smaller than 5% of the surface.
  - *Open ocean*: everything else. It is split at 10 rings (~1100 km) from the nearest plate
    boundary.
- **Sediment**: rock-equivalent thickness (`sed`) as the mass measure; bulk thickness (`bulk`,
  with porosity) for the thicknesses quoted.
- **Abyssal**: sea-floor cells ≥ 5 rings (~550 km) from any non-oceanic crust.
- **Own trench**: a boundary cell where the cell's own plate subducts (kind `t`; the lower plate
  of diffuse zones, `d`, is not counted). The marginal-sea rim's "land" means own-plate cells
  that are not sea floor, so it includes drowned continental shelf.
- Percentiles are the means of per-snapshot percentiles, not pooled values.

One cell is ~110 km across and ~12,450 km². 1% of the sea floor is ~3.0 Mkm².

The ancient sea floor falls into two different populations.

**A. Trapped seas.** Enclosed basins and marginal seas cover ~1.7% of the sea floor (~5 Mkm²).
- They hold ~70% of the ancient sediment, 5–9 km thick.
- Just over half (56–63%) of the sea floor older than 300 Myr is here, including about two thirds
  of the never-recycled initial ocean (≥ 500 Myr). The rest, 0.5–0.7% of the sea floor, is old
  open ocean.
- The enclosed basins are small:
  - 0.12–0.14% of the sea floor sits in regions of fewer than 20 cells (< 0.25 Mkm²);
  - 0.34–0.41% sits in regions of 20–123 cells (< 1.5 Mkm², below 0.3% of the surface).
- This is the population the issue was opened for, and it is unchanged in kind since PR #23.

**B. Old open ocean.** 2.2–4.2% of the sea floor (7–13 Mkm²).
- It lies in large regions (≥ 5% of the surface) and is mostly 200–300 Myr old; 0.47–0.72% of
  the sea floor is open ocean ≥ 300 Myr.
- Its sediment is moderate (1.7–2.9 km mean bulk). Much of that sits near continents (see below).
- This population causes the spikes (ad hoc check of the 12 snapshots above 9%, from the
  per-category counts in `ancient.json`): in 10 of them, open-ocean regions of ≥ 5% of the
  surface are the largest contributor.
  - In 5 of the 12, more than 90% of the ancient floor lies on almost purely oceanic plates
    (< 10% continental crust).
  - It ages because no trench consumes it from the far side. Within 100–200 Myr it is consumed
    again, and the fraction falls back to 2–5%.

Common to both populations:

- 81–84% of the ancient cells are more than 5 rings (> 550 km) from a trench where their own
  plate subducts.
- Only 7–8% belong to plates with no trench at all. The plates do subduct, but at the wrong edge.
- 56–66% of the ancient cells lie within 3 rings (330 km) of non-oceanic crust, and those cells
  hold 83–87% of the ancient sediment.
- The median distance to a plate boundary is 8–9 rings (~900 km).
- The cells sit on large plates: median plate size 20–27% of the surface. 55–68% of the cells
  are on plates with ≥ 10% continental crust.

## Is it plausible for an Earth-like planet?

Earth today has almost no sea floor older than ~180–200 Myr. The exceptions are small remnants
(Ionian Sea, Herodotus Basin, possibly 230–340 Myr, well under 1 Mkm²). Earth's mean sea-floor age
is ~60–64 Myr.

The model's mean age is Earth-like (61–67 Myr), but its age distribution has a heavier old tail.
Taking each population and age band in turn:

- **200–300 Myr old open ocean and passive-margin basement (2.5–4.4% of the sea floor):
  plausible.** Earth's oldest floor is ~180 Myr because the Atlantic and Indian Oceans opened
  ~180 Myr ago, not because sea floor cannot get older. Passive margins on Earth have lived ~180
  Myr on average and some several hundred Myr (Bradley 2008). An Earth-like planet at another
  point of its Wilson cycle can plausibly carry a few percent of 200–300 Myr floor. The spikes to
  10–17% are at the high edge of that and come from single slow plates.
- **Trapped seas with thick fill (1.7%, ~5 Mkm²): the fill is plausible, the age is not.** Earth
  has small trapped basins with 10–20 km of fill (South Caspian, Black Sea, Gulf of Mexico,
  Precaspian), but they are younger than 200 Myr or, like the Precaspian (Paleozoic, basement
  possibly continental), filled to land. The model's defect is that its basins stay open sea for
  300–1000+ Myr.
- **Sea floor ≥ 300 Myr (1.3–1.6%) and especially ≥ 500 Myr (0.3–0.4%, ages up to 1000–1700
  Myr): not plausible.** No Earth-like process keeps open sea floor for 500+ Myr; small basins
  close by rollback into them, by underthrusting, or by burial into continental lithosphere. This
  part is small in area but holds a large part of the trapped sediment.
- **Sediment: partly plausible.** That old margins and basins hold a large share of the oceanic
  sediment is Earth-like: on Earth, too, most oceanic sediment sits on continental rises and in
  marginal basins. But ~40% on 4–6% of the area, at 4–9 km, is concentrated more than on Earth,
  and about half of it (53–57%) sits on floor ≥ 300 Myr old.

## Grid artefacts (#64) versus physics

**Grid or threshold artefacts:** ~0.5–0.7% of the sea floor, a tenth to a sixth of the ancient
area, but much of the oldest part.
- Enclosed specks of fewer than 20 cells (0.12–0.14%) lie below the grid's resolution (earlier
  checks on #14 traced such specks partly to resampling flicker and sutures).
- Enclosed basins of 20–410 cells (0.34–0.41% below 123 cells plus 0.13–0.19% of 123–410 cells)
  are mostly smaller than the smallest subducting piece a split may create (0.6% of the surface, ~250 cells; #23). That minimum exists because the grid
  cannot resolve a slab and a microplate that small. On Earth such basins close by trench
  rollback into them (Calabria), which needs sub-cell slabs.

**Physics or model gaps:**
- Larger marginal seas (0.3–5% of the surface) whose old margins do not initiate subduction.
- Large open oceans whose far side never gets a trench. This is a subduction-initiation-rate
  question: ~0.03 passive-margin initiations per Myr along ~46,000–53,000 km of old
  passive margins (logs, 500–1000 Myr).
  It is the same rate limitation PR #23 and PR #63 found.
- These are resolved by the grid, so #64 does not cover them.

## Answer

- **Still a problem?** Partly. By the issue's own target (≲ 1% ≥ 200 Myr), yes:
  - 3.9–5.9% of the sea floor (11–18 Mkm²) is ≥ 200 Myr old, no better than after PR #23;
  - that floor holds ~40% of the oceanic sediment.
- **Two populations now:**
  1. Old open ocean (2.2–4.2% of the sea floor, mostly 200–300 Myr): the larger part by area.
     It is plausible for an Earth-like planet and should not count as a defect; its ≥ 300 Myr
     tail (0.5–0.7%) is borderline.
  2. Trapped seas: ~1.7% of the sea floor with 5–9 km of fill and ages up to 1000+ Myr. This is
     the real remaining defect: the ≥ 300 Myr relics and the sediment trap.
- **Pelagic blanket:** it exists now on abyssal floor (median 200–300 m), but it is thinner and
  patchier than Earth's ~0.2–0.6 km on old pelagic floor.

## Independent evaluation

An independent agent checked the scripts, recomputed one snapshot from scratch (default seed 3 at
700 Myr: identical to `ancient.json` and the log), reran the summary and found all tabled numbers
matching. Verdict: agree with changes. Adopted: the retargeted threshold (the first draft's
"≥ 300 Myr ≲ 0.3%" could not be met, because the plausible open ocean alone has 0.5–0.7% ≥ 300
Myr), the smaller expected effect of option 3, "just over half" instead of "most" of the ≥ 300 Myr
floor in trapped seas, Earth's trapped basins being younger than 200 Myr (fill plausible, age not),
the pelagic reference (~0.2–0.6 km) and per-seed scatter, the grid-artefact size range up to the
~250-cell piece threshold, and marking the spike and specks claims as ad hoc or from earlier checks.
