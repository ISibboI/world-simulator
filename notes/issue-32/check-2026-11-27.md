# #32 check, 2026-11-27: is sea-floor production still too high?

_`main` at c770e19 (after #195, #211, #182, #190/#192/#193). Measurement only; nothing in the
simulation changed. Earlier investigation: [investigation.md](investigation.md) (2026-11-20,
`main` at cd940a0)._

## Short answer

**Much smaller than when the issue was opened, and now within a plausible range for an
Earth-like planet. Recommendation: close.**

- Sea-floor production is **5.1 km²/yr** (seed means 3.7–6.0), down from 6.0–6.2. That is
  **~1.5× Earth's 3.4–3.5 km²/yr** (PB2002, all diverging boundaries) or ~1.65× Earth's sea-floor
  only 3.0–3.1 (was 1.8–2.0×). Cells actually filled with new sea floor are 5.2–5.4 km²/yr; the
  extra +10% from gap filling is down to +3%.
- The excess is a third in **ridge length** (80–85 thousand km against Earth's 60–67) and two
  thirds in the **full spreading rate** (6.0 against 4.5 cm/yr). Plates' absolute speeds stay
  Earth-like (3.9–4.0 cm/yr); what is fast is the motion of neighbours against each other.
- **Slab mass flux 24.5–24.8 Gt/yr, ~1.35× Earth's ~18** (area flux 4.8 km²/yr); was
  29 Gt/yr / 6.0 km²/yr in the #37 check of 2026-11-26.
- **Mean sea-floor age 66–68 Myr** (oceanic crust, all ages; the log's "sea floor" mean 62–64),
  **52 Myr without the ≥ 200 Myr relict crust of #14** (was 40–41), against Earth's ~60–65. The
  age–area distribution is closer to Earth's triangle than before, but still front-loaded:
  27% younger than 20 Myr (Earth ~21%, before 36%).

## What drives the remaining excess

Production = ridge length × full spreading rate (+6% opening elsewhere). Against Earth's ridges
(PB2002 OSR 67 thousand km × 4.5 cm/yr = 3.0 km²/yr), the model's 4.7–4.8 km²/yr at ridges is
**1.6× = 1.2 (length) × 1.33 (rate)**.

1. **Neighbouring plates move fast against each other (rate, ×1.33).** Absolute speeds are
   Earth-like, but only 13% of the boundary moves slower than 2 cm/yr (Earth 39%) and 78% of it
   is ridge or trench (Earth 46%). As found in the investigation and in
   [realism-batch-2](../realism-batch-2/measurement.md): on Earth half the boundary lies between
   plates without slabs (the Atlantic, Indian-Ocean and Arctic ridges, Africa–Eurasia), where
   boundaries are slow; in the model such plates rarely border each other. #42/#63 (resistance to
   subduction initiation) and #195 (fewer, plume-driven rifts) improved this from 3–6% to 13%
   slow boundary and cut trench length by 30%, but slow diffuse continental boundaries (Earth's
   CRB/CTF/CCB, 77 thousand km) are still rare.
2. **Ridge length (×1.2–1.3).** Fewer plates since #195 (18–19 instead of ~25) shortened the
   ridges from 93 to 80–85 thousand km. Part of what remains is a **grid effect** (#64, accepted):
   transform offsets shorter than a cell (~110 km) cannot exist, so a ridge–transform staircase is
   drawn as one zig-zag ridge, and its length counts as ridge instead of transform (the model has
   24 thousand km of "other" boundary against Earth's 74 of transforms). This shifts length
   between the classes but **does not inflate production**: opening integrated along a boundary
   (∫ v·n dl) is the same for the staircase and the smoothed line. The ×1/1.1 zig-zag factor
   already corrects the length for the cell geometry.
3. **Not drivers:** plate count (fewer plates than Earth, yet more production), absolute plate
   speed (Earth-like), the measurement (kinematic, created and young-area estimates agree within
   10%; the young-area one is lower because ~10% of new sea floor is subducted before 10 Myr),
   gap filling (now +3%).

## Is 1.5× plausible for an Earth-like planet?

Yes, at the upper end:

- Earth's own production has varied: reconstructions give mid-Cretaceous rates ~1.2–1.5× today's
  (Larson 1991; Müller et al. 2008, 2016), though Rowley (2002) argues for a near-constant rate.
  An Earth-like planet with somewhat faster plates is within that range.
- The default run is now epoch 0 (4.0 Ga). A younger planet has a hotter mantle; whether that
  means faster or slower plates is debated (classical parameterised convection gives faster
  plates, Korenaga 2006 argues for slower ones), so either is plausible there. The model has no epoch dependence of mantle heat yet (#208), so the same
  rates are used for the modern preset, where they sit at the high end.
- The consequences are in plausible ranges: mean sea-floor age 52 Myr without the relict tail
  (Earth ~62; a faster-produced, younger ocean floor is shallower and raises the sea level, as
  in Earth's Cretaceous), plate speeds 4 cm/yr.

## Consequences

- **Carbon cycle (#196, merged):** degassing 12–16 Tmol/yr, ~2× Earth's. Ridge degassing scales
  with the new sea floor (×1.5) and the arcs' return of seafloor carbon with the subduction flux
  (×1.4–1.6), so about 1.5× of the 2× comes from here, the rest from the coefficients (each
  uncertain ~2×). φ calibrates it away for the barren preset (0.95–1.57, within its band); for
  the modern preset φ is 3.5–8.2, partly because of this.
- **Slab dynamic topography (#37, closed by #211):** the field scales with the slab mass flux,
  now 1.35× Earth's (was 1.6×). With #211's physical kernels the depressions are already
  plausible; an Earth-like flux would make them ~25% shallower.
- **Sea-floor age (#14):** the relict ≥ 200 Myr crust (5% of the ocean) still lifts the mean age
  by 14–16 Myr; without it the mean (52 Myr) is below Earth's. Halving production would push the
  mean beyond 80 Myr (investigation §5), so the age distribution argues against forcing
  production down to Earth's.
