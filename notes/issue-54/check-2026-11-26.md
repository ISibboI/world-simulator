# #54 check, 2026-11-26: failed rift arms with non-rigid plates

Question (Sebastian): is this still a problem now that we have non-rigid plates?
Checked against `main` at 1418167 (after #163 sheet, #182 stress breakup, #189). Code
reading only.

## Verdict

Still missing (partly emergent at most). The model has no failed arm, and nothing marks the
arms of a plume rift that did not open. The sheet and #182 add two weakening mechanisms near
plumes, but both are discs around the plume, not arms. One of them heals too fast to act as an
aulacogen; the other is permanent only in the break proxy and only where the sheet actually
stretched the crust.

## What exists on main

* **Plume rifts** (`events::rift_above_plumes`, see the #51 note) still split a plate into
  exactly two parts. The arms that did not open are not recorded.
* **Plume head heating** (`weak_zones::reheat_above_plumes`): this resets the thermal age to
  25 Myr over a ~1000 km radius during the head stage (later ~500 km from the tail). Strength
  is √(age/100 Myr), so it is ~0.5 at the centre and recovers fully within ~75 Myr. The
  weakening is carried with the plate (`Crust::reheated`) and is seen by the sheet's viscosity,
  by #182 and by later plume rifts. It is a disc, not an arm, and it heals in ~75 Myr. Real
  aulacogens are reactivated after 100s of Myr: the Benue Trough was inverted ~30 Myr after
  rifting, but the Dniepr–Donets and the Midcontinent Rift were reactivated much later.
* **Sheet stretching** (#163, `transport_crust`): where the sheet stretches continental
  lithosphere, it thins the crust and heats the lithosphere (McKenzie β). Two effects follow:
  * The thinned basement lowers strength in `lithosphere_strength` through the `thinning` term.
    This is **permanent**, and #182 sections and plume-rift cuts see it.
  * The sheet's own strength ignores the thinning on purpose ("passive margins are strong"),
    so this weakness does not feed back into the sheet's deformation.

  A plume therefore leaves a lasting weak zone only where the sheet actually stretched weak
  continental lithosphere. The sheet's only plume loads are drag against the radial outflow and
  weakening by heating, and both are small. The swell GPE is not a load (see the #51 note). So
  this stretching is weak and has no preferred arm.
* **Inherited weakness in #182**: #182 uses `lithosphere_strength`. That includes sutures and
  orogenic belts (`Crust::orogenic`, `SUTURE_WEAKENING` 0.25, healing over 200 Myr), plume and
  stretching resets and thinned crust. There is no term for extensional fault fabric. Shortening
  marks crust as orogenic, but stretching leaves only the thermal reset (which heals) and the
  thinning.

## Proposal (physics-only)

The failed arm should come from the physics, not from a mark that a rule paints three arms with.

1. **Let the plume stretch the lithosphere in the sheet before it breaks.** This needs the swell
   GPE load and the magma-assisted weakening from the #51 proposal. Then the sheet strains the
   plume head along its weakest directions: radial arms that follow sutures and belts. #182 or
   the sheet split opens the arms that run through, and the others stop as stretched zones.
2. **Extensional fabric as inherited weakness.** Shortening already marks crust as orogenic. In
   the same way, record the cumulative extension strain of continental lithosphere (a
   `rifted`/fabric value per sample, mixed by transport like `orogenic`). It weakens both the
   break proxy and the sheet, analogous to `SUTURE_WEAKENING`, and heals over
   `OROGENIC_HEALING_TIME`-like time scales or slower. Aulacogens and failed rifts are then
   long-lived weak zones that later splits (#182, plume rifts) and sheet deformation (inversion
   under compression) reactivate. Rifted margins get the same fabric, which fits Wilson-cycle
   reactivation.
3. Check: on 4–8 seeds, count stretched-but-unbroken zones next to plume rifts, how long they
   stay weak, and how often later breaks or thrusting follow them. Look at the strength view at
   a plume rift.

**Effort:** step 2 alone takes about 1 subagent day: a new crust field, transport mixing, a
strength term and a seed comparison. Step 1 is shared with #51 (2–3 days). Without step 1 the
fabric marks only what the sheet stretches now, which near plumes is little. So the issue is
best done after or together with #51.

**Grid (#64):** a failed arm is a graben ~50–100 km wide, which is a single cell at ~110 km.
In the model it would be a 1–3-cell-wide weak band. That is fine for where later breaks run,
but the graben itself (sediment fill, flanks) is not resolved.
