# #33 review of plan and commit 7d26a11

_2026-11-21, independent review. Read the plan, the diff and `src/temperature.rs` (`update`), `src/climate.rs` (`month_inputs`), `src/planet.rs`. No runs._

**Verdict: approve with changes.** The free-air shift fixes the tropical case cleanly and is bit-identical to `main` at `free_air_height = 0`. As written it also applies at high latitudes, where the physics behind it is wrong, and the validation does not yet check that.

## Findings, by importance

1. **High-latitude plateaus and ice sheets: the assumption is reversed there.** A large cold plateau sets its own surface temperature by radiative cooling under a strong inversion. The Antarctic plateau at 3 km averages about −50 °C, while the free air at 3 km over the Southern Ocean is about −20 °C. Greenland's summit is about 10 K colder than the free air at its height. Pepin & Seidel (2005) is about mountain stations, not ice sheets, and WTG (Sobel et al. 2001) only holds where the Coriolis parameter is small (|φ| ≲ 20–30°). The code applies `w(z)` at every latitude (temperature.rs:640, :897–902). Polar band (62.7–90°N; values above the band centre at 70.8° are clamped, temperature.rs:863–866) with polar land at −25 °C, 3 km: the old formula gives −44.5 °C. The new one gives T_free − 19.5: −39.5 if the band's sea is under sea ice at −20 °C, −21.3 for open water at freezing, −15.5 for a +4 °C polar sea. All still have full ice (full at −15 °C, temperature.rs:1072, :1089), but the margin shrinks from ~30 K to 0.5–6 K. A few K of warming (greenhouse forcing, solar constant, a gyre bringing warm water into the band) then removes ice sheets that should survive. The ice is also no longer tied to its own albedo and elevation feedback, so ice sheets lose their hysteresis. **Change:** fade the weight with latitude (for example `w × clamp((45° − |φ|)/15°, 0, 1)`, full up to 30°, zero beyond 45°), or apply it only where it warms *and* |φ| < ~40°. That keeps what the issue asks for and leaves polar ice to the local balance.
2. **Hot continents get colder highlands, and can gain ice.** Where land is warmer than the band's sea, the shift is negative. Example: subtropical land at 30 °C, band sea at 22 °C, 3 km: 2.5 °C instead of 10.5 °C. At 4.6 km, −8 °C gives ice cover ≈ 0.3 where `main` had none (if there is precipitation). Real high plateaus (Tibet, Altiplano) are *warmer* than the free air at their height, because they are elevated heat sources. **Change:** at least, report ice and temperature for subtropical land above 4 km in the validation. Better, take `max(0, excess)` for the shift. It cures the runaway, which is a cold bias only, and it cannot create ice.
3. **The diagnosis is incomplete: one coarse cell cannot run away on its own, and the 15 °C start matters.** `coupling = 0.05·2/(3·0.069²) ≈ 7 W/m²/K` per neighbour, so ≈ 42 W/m²/K to the six neighbours against B = 2.09 (temperature.rs:484–485, :743–757). One fully iced coarse cell (−120 W/m²) cools only about 3 K. The diffusion length √(D/B) ≈ 0.155 rad ≈ 1000 km, so the runaway needs iced high ground that spans several coarse cells (the test plateau is 16°×24°). Second contributor: the spin-up starts with `previous_annual_land = 15 °C` and excess 0 (temperature.rs:456–457). A 4.5 km plateau then sits at −14 °C (cover 0.93) in year 1, in `main` and in the new code alike. `main` keeps model state across updates (`spun_up`), so a glaciated state that holds itself up can persist for the whole run. That likely explains the −6.4 K abiotic start (inferred). **Change:** in the plan, replace "single coarse cells" with "high ground ≳ 1000 km across" and name the initial state. As a check, run the unit test with `free_air_height = 0` and the land initialised at 30 °C. If the plateau stays ice-free, `main` has two equilibria and initialisation is part of the bug.
4. **The 5% sea threshold is a step** (temperature.rs:853). When a band moves across it, the free air jumps from the mean of all surfaces to the mean of a few sea cells, possibly sea-ice or inland cells below sea level. That can be 20–30 K in a polar band. Continents drift, so a run will cross it. **Change:** blend smoothly, e.g. `(Σ sea·T_sea + ε·Σ T_air) / (Σ sea + ε·n)` with ε ≈ 0.05, or a linear ramp from 0 to 10% sea.
5. **Sea-ice bands.** `seasonal_ocean` under ice is the ice-top temperature (temperature.rs:790–798), so the "free air" there is the cold surface air below the inversion. With `inversion_strength = 0` this errs cold, which is safe for ice. Document it. It is one more reason the method is a tropical one.
6. **Verified OK in code:**
   - Band centres at `(b+½)/n` in sin φ, clamped beyond the outermost centres (temperature.rs:863–868): correct. The sea weights are per coarse cell, which is fine because cells have near-equal area.
   - At `free_air_height = 0`, `highland_weight` returns exactly 0, `x + 0.0 − y == x − y`, and the excess is always finite (`weight.max(1e-9)`), so the results are bit-identical. Only the removed comment differs.
   - At w = 1 the output annual mean equals `interp(regional) − Γz` exactly, because `monthly_land` and `previous_annual_land` are the same year. The albedo loop uses last year's values, as the old ice logic did.
   - Energy: the shift is diagnostic. It only changes which ice the coarse land albedo sees. Nothing is double counted, but nothing is shared either: where highland ice remains, its coarse cell still bears the full deficit. The plan says so; drop its earlier claim that the deficit is "shared by the region".
   - The seasonal shift is constant. The highland keeps the continental amplitude around a free-air mean. Acceptable.
7. **climate.rs effects (verified where they come in):**
   - `sea_level = T + Γz` (climate.rs:498–500) now returns the free-air value for high cells. That is what the column and thermal-wind code should see: the zonal mean of the free troposphere and no spurious cold pool over plateaus. The plateau-edge gradients in the thermal wind change accordingly.
   - Land potential evaporation uses the surface temperature directly (climate.rs:556–565), so warmer highlands raise PET, and drier runoff is possible.
   - climate.rs has no snow; ice is `fields.ice` only (render.rs:58, planet.rs:410).
8. **Unit test:** it is meaningful for the off case (runaway reproduced). For the on case it is close to circular: at 4.5 km, w = 1, so the reduced plateau equals the interpolated band sea by construction. **Add:**
   - (a) A polar continent with a 3 km plateau that must keep ice cover > 0.9, including with the polar band's sea open.
   - (b) A mid-latitude (45°) plateau: the change stays within a few K of the lapse-rate value.
   - (c) A subtropical hot-continent plateau that gains no ice.
   - (d) Continuity: free air changes by less than 1 K as the band's sea fraction crosses 5%.
   - (e) A warm-start `main` case (finding 3).

## Guard rails for the validation (both settings, same seeds)
- Ice-sheet area poleward of 60° and the land area with annual mean < −10 °C there. Target: change below ~10% unless explained.
- Mean annual temperature of polar land (> 70°) and of land above 2 km poleward of 50°.
- Ice cover and temperature of subtropical land (15–35°) above 3.5 km (finding 2).
- `solar_sweep` (temperature.rs:1339) on dumps of 2–3 seeds: frozen share along 100→90→100%. It must not reach a snowball earlier, and the hysteresis loop width should be reported. Also +4 W/m² `greenhouse_forcing`: the polar ice loss must not be abrupt.
- Abiotic t = 0 offset with and without the change, plus one run with the spin-up initialised warm, to separate the initial state from the feedback.
