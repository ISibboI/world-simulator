## Plan for #30: plume heads weaken the lithosphere; the junction emerges from the cut

**Today (#23).** A plume rift prescribes three arms 120° apart (one towards the nearest continent ±30°). For each pair of arms, `Break::Junction` places rigid blocks around the 120° lune and the remaining 240°; lines of forced cells ("spines", 0.4 rad long) along the bisectors and sector-forced neighbours of the plume cell tie the break to the plume. The weakest pair that leaves both parts large enough wins. The ~120° angle at the plume is therefore imposed by the arms and spines.

**Physics.** A plume head (~2000 km across once flattened below the plate; Campbell & Griffiths 1990; White & McKenzie 1989) heats and thins the lithosphere above it. The classic swell model reheats the lithosphere to the thermal state of ~25 Myr old lithosphere at the centre ("thermal rejuvenation"; Detrick & Crough 1978; Crough 1983), tapering off towards the edge of the head. Thinner, hotter lithosphere is weaker; on Earth, plume-related break-up runs through the weakened dome and along inherited weak zones nearby (Afar; Paraná–Etendeka; East African Rift around the Tanzania craton, Koptev et al. 2015). Whether the two arms that open meet at ~120° (Afar, Benue/South Atlantic) or form one nearly straight rift (Deccan/Seychelles, Central Atlantic) depends on the weak structure around the dome, so the angle should be an outcome, not an input.

**Change.**
1. `weak_zones.rs`: lithosphere above a plume head is thermally rejuvenated. Strength stands for mechanical thickness, so the profile is defined on thickness: at the centre the lithosphere is as thick as 25 Myr old lithosphere (√(25/100) = 0.5 of full), and the thinning tapers with the shape of a flattened head, `1 − (d/R)²`, back to the unaffected thickness exactly at `R = PLUME_HEAD_RADIUS` (~1000 km, 0.157 rad). The result is `min(own thermal term, 1 − (1 − 0.5)·(1 − (d/R)²)·g)`, where `g` is the head's arrival (the same growth ramp as `Plume::flux`, 0 → 1 over the first 10 Myr), so heads that have not arrived yet do not weaken; decaying plumes keep their head below the plate until they die (thermal relaxation is slower than the plume's 20 Myr fade). One radius over the lifetime: the plume-head stage is what matters for rifting (rifts happen at plume age ≥ 20 Myr); the later, narrower tail swell (~500 km, Hawaii) is ignored. The field is tied to the plume's mantle position, not carried with the plate (simplification, stated). Thinning by stretching and the suture factor still multiply. `lithosphere_strength` takes the plume cap as an argument (stays testable without a `Simulation`); `weak_zone_cut` precomputes one strength per cell per cut. Applies to every weak-zone cut (rifts, margin and fracture-zone initiations) — a property of the lithosphere; initiation metrics are reported.
2. `events.rs`: a plume rift uses a plain reference great circle through the plume with the existing band/rigid-block logic and recuts. Instead of a random orientation within ±30° of the nearest continent, the orientation is chosen by the cut itself: 6 orientations through the plume at 30° steps, the weakest break that leaves both parts large enough wins (else the weakest; the recut logic handles it). The nearby continent stays passable (may be cut) but is not forced to be cut. Remove `Break::Junction`, the three arms, `SPINE_LENGTH` and the sector-forced plume neighbours. The break runs through the plume only where that is the weakest path.
3. Diagnostics: `junction_rifts` (printed in `main.rs`) becomes `head_rifts`, "rifts whose new boundary passes within half the head radius of the plume". Offline, from the `New plate boundary` debug lines: the distribution of the boundary's minimum distance to the plume (median, quartiles), the angle at the plume (angle between boundary points ±6 cells ≈ 660 km along the trace from the point nearest the plume) for boundaries passing within R/2, and whether the plume cell is continental or oceanic.
4. Tests: remove the lune test; add (a) strength above a plume head: 0.5 at the centre for old lithosphere, unaffected outside `R`, young lithosphere not strengthened; (b) a plume rift in uniform lithosphere passes through the plume (existing test, now without spines); (c) a plume inside a continent with two old sutures leaving the head at ~120° to each other (inside the band): the break passes through the head and its angle at the plume is within ~100–140° (emergent junction).

**Validation (8 seeds × 1000 Myr, main vs branch, same seeds).** Rifts per run; share of rifts through the plume head; angle at the plume (median, quartiles, share < 150°); rifts on sutures; great-circle splits (must stay 0); recut splits; sea floor ≥ 200 Myr (%), mean sea-floor age; trench/ridge/collision length; plates; runtime (seed 2 concurrently).

**Acceptance criteria (set before the runs).** Great-circle splits stay 0; a majority of rifts pass within R/2 of the plume; the angle is no longer a fixed value (spread, reported as is); sea-floor age, ≥ 200 Myr share, trench/ridge/collision length, plate count and initiations within seed noise of `main` (|t| < ~2.4) or explained; runtime ≤ +10%.

**Expectations / risks.** (Review: approve with changes; all adopted — thickness-based taper ending at R, gating on head arrival, precomputed strengths, quantitative junction test, distance distribution and acceptance criteria, orientation by min cut. The reviewer also notes heat-flow data suggest oceanic swells are reheated less than the rejuvenation model says, Von Herzen et al. 1989; continental heads such as Afar clearly thin the lithosphere.) The median angle will rise above main's imposed 122°, probably with a broad spread: in uniform lithosphere the cut through an isotropically weakened dome between two half-plate blocks is straight. Junction-like corners appear where weak zones leave the dome at an angle. Without spines, some breaks may bypass the plume along a weaker zone nearby (young sea floor, margins: strength 0.05–0.25 < 0.5); that is physical (East Africa) but is reported. Not included: dome uplift (dynamic topography is #21) and an explicit anisotropic hoop-tension term (radial cracks on the dome flanks), whose magnitude relative to lithospheric strength is poorly constrained and would be a tuning knob; listed as a follow-up.

## Revision after the first 8-seed run (weakening alone)

With the plume head weakening alone (v1), the cut missed the plume in most rifts: only 25% of the new boundaries passed within 500 km of the plume (`main`: 65%), median distance 1576 km. The minimum cut knows where the lithosphere is weak, but not where the driving force acts; a 0.5× head is no stronger an attractor than a suture, and much weaker than young sea floor or a rifted margin. A rift 1500 km from its plume is not driven by it.

The plume head pushes the lithosphere on its two sides apart, so the break nucleates at the plume (Burke & Dewey 1973). v2 therefore ties the break to the plume with two lines of cells, from the plume perpendicular to the reference circle into the two parts. Unlike #23, these lines do not lie on the bisectors of a prescribed 120° lune. The arms may leave the plume in any direction between them, and the six circle orientations are searched, so the angle at the plume is not prescribed. The unit test checks angles of 90°, 120° and 150° along sutures.
