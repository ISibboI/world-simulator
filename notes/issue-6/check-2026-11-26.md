# Issue #6 check, 2026-11-26: young oceanic crust at convergent boundaries

Question (Sebastian, 2026-11-26): is this still a problem? **Verdict: fixed** (below the < 5% target), closed.

## How new oceanic crust is made on main (ad59218)

Only `resolve_ownership` (`src/tectonics/boundaries.rs`) creates age-0 crust (`Term::NewSeaFloor`):

1. **Spreading**: a gap cell where the receiving plate opens away from a nearby plate
   (`opens_away`: ≥ `min_spreading_rate`, smoothed normals over 3 rings, obliquity limit).
   Thick continental crust there is stretched instead (`stretch_rift`).
2. **Open sea floor inside a plate**: a gap away from other plates whose slot was consumed and
   has too few crust neighbours to heal.
3. Degenerate restart (all crust destroyed).

All other gaps (resampling gaps at trenches and transforms) are healed **without creating
crust**: `heal_gap` fills a slot with a 1/(k+1) share of each neighbour (mass-conserving), or the
cell shows the adjacent sample (`shared_cells`; no new mass). Slivers where the plate's crust was
consumed show the adjacent sample. So the "copies create volume" part of the old status is gone:
the copies are now shares. The thin viscous sheet (#163) adds no crust either (finite-volume
transport, ledger change ~1e-8 Gt per step).

**The remaining young crust at convergent boundaries is mostly not made there:**

- Its ages are skewed older than ridge crust: 30% younger than 1 Myr (ridge cells: 44%), median
  1.5 Myr (ridge: 1.0). Crust made at the boundary in the last step would be < 1 Myr old. It
  is mostly ridge crust that reached a convergent boundary: ridges approaching trenches,
  ridge–trench triple junctions and plate reorganisations (on Earth too, e.g. the Chile Rise
  and the Juan de Fuca system, very young crust is subducted).
- 41% of these cells also border a plate moving away from them, i.e. they sit at triple
  junctions where a ridge meets a convergent boundary. At ~110 km cells such a junction is one
  or two cells wide, so a ridge cell is automatically "at" the convergent boundary of the third
  plate. This is a grid effect (#64).
- Their median convergence is 2.6 cm/yr (58% > 2 cm/yr), mostly trenches and "other"
  (slowly closing or oblique) boundaries.
