# #51 check, 2026-11-26: plume rifts with non-rigid plates

Question (Sebastian): is this still a problem now that we have non-rigid plates?
Checked against `main` at 1418167 (after #163 sheet, #182 stress breakup, #189).
Code reading only; no runs were needed (numbers below are order-of-magnitude estimates).

## Verdict

Still relevant, unchanged, and still low priority. Non-rigid plates did not touch plume
rifting: plume rifts on `main` still go through the old minimum cut with the two nucleation
lines (the kinematic stand-in). Since PR #74, these rifts form at the plume, so the problem is
how the model is formulated, not wrong results.

## Current code path (main)

Each step, `plate_events` (src/tectonics/events.rs) runs three independent split rules in
this order:

1. `rift_above_plumes`: a plume older than `events.rift_delay` (20 Myr) under a plate with
   continental crust nearby (or under a large plate) calls `split_locally(…, SplitAnchor::Rift)`.
   That calls `weak_zone_cut` (src/tectonics/weak_zones.rs) for 6 orientations of a great
   circle through the plume. The *forced* cells are the nucleation lines: cells within one edge
   of the line perpendicular to the circle, up to `SPINE_LENGTH` = 0.4 rad (~2600 km) from the
   plume, tied to the two sides. This is exactly the PR #50 mechanism.
2. `split_deforming_plates` (sheet, #163): splits a plate when its rigid blocks move apart
   across deforming lithosphere faster than `sheet.split_speed` for `sheet.split_time`.
3. `break_overloaded_plates` (#182, src/tectonics/breakup.rs): a section whose limit-analysis
   overload has stayed > 1 for 5 Myr breaks.

The sheet and #182 know about plumes only in two indirect ways:

* **Strength.** Plume heating resets the thermal age, `Crust::reheated`
  (`weak_zones::reheat_above_plumes`). Over the plume head (radius ~1000 km) this lowers the
  relative strength to ~0.5. The sheet's viscosity and #182's section strength
  (`lithosphere_strength`) both see this.
* **Loads.** The mantle flows radially away from the plume (`mantle.rs`, a point source softened
  within `core_radius` 0.1 rad). The sheet's `cell_forces` include basal drag at the rigid
  velocity against this flow. So the plume's outflow already enters #182's load set as drag.

What is **not** in the loads is the plume swell's gravitational potential energy: `cell_forces`
has ridge push and the GPE of thickened continental crust, but not the dynamic topography
(`MantleFlow::dynamic_topography`, swell up to 650 m), although that field is already computed.
#182's sections also do not look for a cut through the plume. They are the level sets of
distance from three far-apart cells of the plate, refined within 3 cells.

## Could the plume's tension simply enter #182's load set?

Partly it already does (the drag), and the size of the loads tells us what is missing. Rough
numbers with the defaults (`basal_drag` 10¹⁵ Pa·s/m, ×4 under continents; plume flux
2–6 × 10⁻³ m²/s; `breakup.strength` F = 5 × 10¹³ N/m):

* **Outflow drag** on one half of a plate across a section through the plume:
  ≈ c·Q·R·Θ/π, with Θ ~ 0.5 rad as the half-size of the plate. Per metre of section this is
  ~10¹¹–10¹² N/m (oceanic to continental).
* **Swell GPE** of a ~650 m swell: ~ρ_m·g·h·H with H ~ 100 km, i.e. ~10¹² N/m. That is the
  same order as ridge push (3 × 10¹²).
* **Section strength** at the plume: F·s ≈ 2.5 × 10¹³ N/m (s ≈ 0.5 after head heating).

So the plume's own loads give an overload of only **~0.05–0.2**. With honest loads, #182 alone
would almost never rift at a plume. This agrees with rifting mechanics: the driving forces
(~10¹²–10¹³ N/m) are too small to break lithosphere that has not been weakened. Buck (2004, 2006)
showed that magma-assisted rifting (dykes fed by the plume) reduces the force needed to rift
by about an order of magnitude, to ~10¹² N/m. In nature, the plume's magma makes plume rifting
possible, not just the push of its head. The model has no such term, so the nucleation lines
are currently what stands in for it.

A plume-stress-weighted cut cost, as proposed in the issue, would fix where the rift runs but
not whether it happens: the timing would still be a rule (`rift_delay`). The physics-only
route is to let #182 decide both.

## Proposal (physics-only replacement of the nucleation lines)

1. **Swell GPE as a load**: add the gradient of the dynamic topography to `cell_forces`, like the
   crustal GPE: force ≈ −ρ_m·g·h·H·∇h per area. The field exists already. This load also
   carries the dome's hoop tension: under a radial push the section with the most tension runs
   through the dome's centre.
2. **Magma-assisted weakening**: inside the plume head, while the plume is in its head stage or
   its flux is high, multiply #182's section strength by a factor of ~0.1–0.2 (Buck 2004). This
   can be shaped by the plume's flux, like `plume_heating_age`. The sheet's viscosity could use
   the same factor.
3. **Sections through plumes**: add each active plume's cell as an extra seed of #182's distance
   level sets, or add great circles through the plume as candidate sections, refined along the
   weakest path as now.
4. Then remove `rift_above_plumes`, `SplitAnchor::Rift`, `SPINE_LENGTH` and `RIFT_ORIENTATIONS`.
   Plume rifts become #182 breakups by opening, with no delay rule.
5. Compare 8 seeds × 1000 Myr with `main`: plume rifts per 100 Myr (main ~2–3), how far the
   break is from the plume, whether supercontinents break up (Wilson cycles), and the count of
   continental rifts.

**Effort:** about 2–3 subagent days including the seed comparison (medium–high: plume rifts
drive the supercontinent cycle, so tuning the magma factor is the risky part). Without step 2,
steps 1 and 3 alone would mostly remove plume rifts.

**Grid (#64):** the plume head is ~18 cells across at ~110 km cells. Sections through it are
resolved to a cell, and the dome's hoop stress is smeared over a few cells. This is fine for
where a rift runs and when it happens, but individual dyke swarms or rift segments are not
resolved.
