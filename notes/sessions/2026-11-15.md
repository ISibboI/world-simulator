### Tectonic plate simulation (first version)

Design: rigid plates on an icosphere (subdivision 6 = 40,962 cells by default), each storing crust
in its own local frame; world cells are assigned to plates each substep (see SUMMARY.md for the
architecture). Substeps are chosen so plates move at most half a cell per substep.

Iterations during development:

1. **Per-cell buoyancy resolution** (winner = most buoyant crust at each cell): plates fragmented
   into many interleaved islands, mountains grew to ~30 km and continental area fell from 30% to
   13% in 500 Myr.
2. Fixes:
   - Subduction polarity decided per plate pair and remembered (forgotten after 20 Myr without
     contact); flips when continental crust arrives at an oceanic overriding plate. Plates became
     coherent.
   - Uplift saturates towards 9 km (max elevation now ~6 km).
   - Continental collisions apply drag between the plates' angular velocities; random plate
     reorganisations restart motion. Continental area now 30% → ~24% in 500 Myr.
   - Single-cell interior holes (resampling artifacts) are filled with copies of neighbouring
     crust instead of fresh oceanic crust.
   - Ocean depth curve made continuous (min of half-space cooling and plate model).
3. Plate speeds raised to 0.004–0.016 rad/Myr (≈ 2.5–10 cm/yr on Earth) because mean ocean age
   kept rising; mean speed is now ~3–4.5 cm/yr, mean ocean age still drifts to ~145 Myr.

A 500 Myr default run takes ~35 s (a single 337 s run was due to machine load; per-substep cost
was verified to stay constant at ~35 ms).

Open issues and next-step ideas are listed in SUMMARY.md.
