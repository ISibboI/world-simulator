### Tectonic plate simulation (first version)

Design: rigid plates on an icosphere (subdivision 6 = 40,962 cells by default), each storing crust
in its own local frame; world cells are assigned to plates each substep (see SUMMARY.md for the
architecture). Substeps are chosen so plates move at most half a cell per substep.

Iterations during development:

1. **Per-cell buoyancy resolution** (winner = most buoyant crust at each cell): plates fragmented
   into many interleaved islands, mountains grew to ~30 km and continental area fell from 30% to
   13% in 500 Myr.
2. Fixes:
   - Subduction polarity decided per plate pair and remembered (forgotten after 20 Myr without
     contact); flips when continental crust arrives at an oceanic overriding plate. Plates became
     coherent.
   - Uplift saturates towards 9 km (max elevation now ~6 km).
   - Continental collisions apply drag between the plates' angular velocities; random plate
     reorganisations restart motion. Continental area now 30% → ~24% in 500 Myr.
   - Single-cell interior holes (resampling artifacts) are filled with copies of neighbouring
     crust instead of fresh oceanic crust.
   - Ocean depth curve made continuous (min of half-space cooling and plate model).
3. Plate speeds raised to 0.004–0.016 rad/Myr (≈ 2.5–10 cm/yr on Earth) because mean ocean age
   kept rising; mean speed is now ~3–4.5 cm/yr, mean ocean age still drifts to ~145 Myr.

A 500 Myr default run takes ~35 s (a single 337 s run was due to machine load; per-substep cost
was verified to stay constant at ~35 ms).

Open issues and next-step ideas are listed in SUMMARY.md.

### Water cycle and erosion

User asked to "assume a water cycle similar to earth" and add erosion.

- New modules `climate.rs` (winds, moisture transport, precipitation), `erosion.rs` (drainage,

    level, and dropping the rest (deep ocean).

  elevation, see SUMMARY.md.

### Shelf check and independent physics review (fb5ffb6)

- User asked whether the flat light-blue areas around continents are sediment. Measured at
  500 Myr (seed 0): 3111 cells between −200 and 0 m (7.6% of the surface); 2933 of them carry
  >500 m of sediment, 2761 more than 6 km, mean 11.4 km; only 364 are continental crust. Without
  their sediment they would lie at a median of −5900 m. So they are sediment piles on oceanic
  crust, built by the shelf fill (up to 256 cells per river mouth, filled to −120 m at once).
  Real shelves are thinned continental crust with 1–5 km of sediment.
- Independent review agent (did not read notes/) ranked findings:
  1. Ocean floor far too old (mean 117–175 Myr vs 64; 22% of surface at −6000…−6400 m), plates
     only die (12 → 5–10): random kinematic plates, no slab pull, no subduction initiation of
     old ocean, no plate splitting/merging. Suggests force-based velocities, initiation at
     >150–200 Myr ocean, rifting/suturing; GDH1 depth curve (asymptote 5651 m).
  2. Sea level fixed at 0 → ocean water volume not conserved (1.75–2.35e9 km³ vs 1.335e9);
     solve sea level for constant water volume.
  3. Continental crust not conserved with erosion (−36% seed 0, −60% seed 3): bedrock→sediment
     1:1 by volume despite density (16% mass loss), deep-sea leftover deleted, sediment
     subduction 3.3 km³/yr (Earth 1–1.6) with no accretion, 75 km cap deletes in long
     collisions, duplicate cells drop erosion changes (`apply_surface_changes`), hole filling
     creates crust.
  4. Thermal uplift applied to continental/arc crust that inherited young oceanic ages → thick
     young crust 1–1.5 km too high (max cells 8.4–11.4 km); 70 km crust gives 6.3 km vs ~5 km.
  5. Gap filling creates new ridge crust at convergent boundaries (28–30% of crust < 3 Myr);
     should only form where plates diverge.
  6. Erosion: implicit solve ignores isostatic response (big rivers erode up to ~6× less than
     their law); runoff = precipitation despite 50% recycling; no endorheic basins.
  7. Shelves are sediment on oceanic crust (matches the shelf check); no rifted margins.
  8. Precipitation too low at mid-latitudes (land 0.3–0.35 vs 0.6 m/yr; half of land < 0.25);
     ocean moisture reset to local evaporation (no fetch, P ≠ E); polar front at 55° too far
     poleward.
  9. Poles ~10 K too warm; land heat capacity too small (use 2–3e7); subtropical seasonality
     too large.
  Acceptable simplifications: local Airy isostasy (no flexure/trenches), constant densities,
  age-only ocean depth, nearest-neighbour resampling, diffusive crustal flow, n=1 stream power,
  fixed winds, zonal EBM, Budyko ice. Minor: land counts `> 0` in statistics vs `>= 0` elsewhere.
  Reproduction scripts and logs: scratchpad `review/` (not durable).

### Runtime optimisation: incremental plate matching

User asked (before the issue 1 tuning) for a profile and a list of optimisations
(`research/profiling-2026-11-15.md`). The plate–cell lookup in `resolve_ownership` took 75–83% of
the run (substeps × plates × cells × ~85 ns). User decisions: **no multi-core for now**
(efficiency first); replace the per-plate bounding cap by **incremental matching**, independent
of plate speed but assuming plates usually move less than a cell per substep, falling back to a
full scan otherwise; keep a debug mode that compares against the full scan; rigorous unit tests
that both modes give identical results.

Implemented in `src/tectonics/matching.rs`:

- Each plate keeps `crust_cells` (cells where it had crust after the last matching) and
  `matched_rotation`. If it rotated less than one mean edge angle since then, only
  `crust_cells` plus 3 rings around them are searched; otherwise (and for new, split or merged
  plates, `crust_cells = None`) a full scan.
- `crust_cells` = searched or gap-filled cells where the plate now has crust, **plus the
  nearest cell of every crust sample that no cell matched** (hidden samples, e.g. overridden
  crust awaiting consumption). Without the latter, hidden samples surfaced outside the search
  set and the modes diverged after 6–48 steps. Ring count 3 follows from geometry: matched
  sample ≤ ~0.6 spacings from its cell, movement < 1 spacing → ≤ ~2.2 spacings; 3 rings reach
  ≥ ~2.6.
- Matches are counting-sorted by cell, stable in plate order, so candidate order is unchanged.
- `--verify-matching` (`MatchingParams::verify`): looks up the unsearched cells too and counts
  crust found there as mismatches (log line "plate matching: … mismatches").
- Tests (icosphere 4, 10 plates): bitwise-identical state (owners, rotations, velocities, all
  crust samples, polarities, mass) and zero mismatches every step for seeds 0–3 × 60 steps, for
  fast plates (3 cells per substep, low drag → full-scan fallback), and with frequent
  splits/merges; searched-cell fraction < 0.4.

Result (seed 0, 500 Myr, default 40,962 cells): **414 s → 168 s (2.5×)**, all 100 log lines
identical apart from the run time; 70,762 incremental matchings vs 177 full scans, ~0.1 searched
cells per plate and cell, 0 mismatches.

Post-change perf profile (200 Myr, self time): `Simulation::step` (inlined substep work: aging,
additions, ledger, gap filling) 28%, `update_crust_cells` 14% (scans every sample of every plate
for unmatched ones — next cheap target: track hidden samples instead of scanning), `local_vertex`
9%, `match_plates` 8%, temperature 3.6%, precipitation 2.9%, `HashMap<Ownership>` hashing 5.7%
(SipHash; a faster hasher or a vec would help), mantle flow evaluation 2.5%, `sincos` 2.7%.
